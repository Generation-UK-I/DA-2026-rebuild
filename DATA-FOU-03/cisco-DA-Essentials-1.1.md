Suddenly, it seems analytics are everywhere.

This transformation to data driven decision making has been in the works for quite a while.

One highly publicized example comes from 2002 when the Oakland A's baseball team general manager used an analysis of data collected over past seasons to choose players and lineups for his team. He took a team of previously undervalued players onto clinch a championship, illustrating the practical value of data analysis.

A more recent example of how data analysis has become crucial to our very existence is the tracking of the spread of the coronavirus pandemic variants.

But the real explosion in analytics today is driven primarily by the tsunami of data triggered by the rise of social media platforms and the Internet of things.

IoT refers to physical objects that capture and transmit data.

Examples of IoT devices include your smartphone and fitness tracker, or even an airplane jet engine that generates one terabyte of data per flight. To organizations, all this data is the new oil. I.e. Like crude oil, data is valuable, but not in its raw form.

Crude oil must be changed to gasoline, plastic, chemicals, and other substances to create a valuable product.

It is the same with data.

In order to move from raw data to insights about organizational decisions, data must be gathered, interpreted, and analyzed, to uncover meaningful patterns. This transformation is the responsibility of data science professionals, especially data analysts.

For businesses, insights derived from analytics will create an environment that can foster transformative changes, and products, processes, and human interactions.

The World Economic Forum identified data science as a global issue that affects every facet of our lives. Stating, "The data revolution creates endless opportunities to confront the grand challenges of the 21st century. Yet, as the scale and scope of data grow, so must our ability to analyze and contextualize it."

The field of data science and data analytics in particular has become an exciting and rewarding career choice. Analysts work in almost every industry, healthcare, governmental agencies, and humanitarian organizations. Data analysts often advance into other areas of data science, including artificial intelligence, machine learning, automation, and sophisticated robotics.

What began with a small number of organizations using analytics to uncover insights into customer and employee behaviors has become an outright revolution in how business decisions are made. Until recently, data analytics involved looking at historical data to uncover trends, patterns and other descriptive information about an organization. Data sets used for this type of analysis—called descriptive analytics—contain static data such as customer transactions, inventory levels, or defect reports. This type of data is not usually analyzed immediately; in fact, most of it is compiled and analyzed on a monthly or quarterly basis.

The primary goal of traditional data analysis is to present information in a way that is easy for non-technical decision makers to understand. The results of descriptive analytics are highly accurate because the data usually doesn’t change during the analysis. Examples of insights uncovered through traditional analytics include past product performance, company profitability, inventory lead times, and advertising program success.

While traditional descriptive analytics still play an important role in decision-making support for all types of organizations, it is no longer enough only to run descriptive analysis on data from the previous fiscal year. To remain competitive, organizations must be able to use data analytics to react quickly to current conditions and make reliable predictions of future behaviors. This is where next-generation analytics comes in.

Advances in the collection, storage and retrieval of real-time data make possible the analysis of all types of data from many sources, such as documents, images, entries in social media or e-commerce sites, and even sensors inside home appliances.

Due to the vast amount of data now being collected, new insights can be gained from analyses to inform more effective decision making. Examples of organizations using advanced analytics to improve their operations include:

A bank immediately analyzing each account transaction to identify potentially fraudulent activity.
A manufacturer constantly monitoring defect reports in order to modify production processes as quickly as possible.
A retailer instantly responding to customer reviews and adjusting products or sales strategies accordingly.
Now, rather than relying on management instinct and past performance, organizations can use real-time data to achieve rapid, transformative change in their operations and processes. Data analysts and other data science professionals are at the forefront of this revolution!

Insights about the market and customers are essential for organizations to succeed.

However, today's environment is constantly changing and making it difficult for organizations to predict their next move.

This is where data analytics comes in.

Data analytics helps organizations to reach better decisions and answer questions like:

- Who are our customers and how do we best reach them?
- What is the state of our market and our competitors?
- What has happened in the past that can inform future growth?
- What is currently happening?
- What does the future hold for our business?
- Why did something occur?

There are four key types of data analytics used to answer these and other difficult questions. These are:

- Descriptive analytics which focuses on what has happened.
- Predictive analytics, which focuses on what could happen.
- Prescriptive analytics, which focuses on what should happen.
- Diagnostic analytics, which focuses on why things happen.

The best type of data analytics for a business will depend on the problem that needs to be solved and the questions that need to be answered.

Some types of data analytics only provide the insights to make reactive, not proactive business decisions. Others are more sophisticated solutions that combine with machine learning capabilities, help organizations make better decisions, and determine market trends and opportunities.

Using data analytics with proactive futurecasting capabilities can help us uncover hidden patterns and gain otherwise unknown insights.
